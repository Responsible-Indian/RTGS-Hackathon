The Challenge

When Damage Becomes the Warning

Across Andhra Pradesh's vital agricultural landscape, a critical gap exists between the onset of crop threats and their detection -- allowing preventable losses to silently accumulate in fields of paddy, cotton, chilli, and red gram.

01

The Silent Spread

Pest infestations and disease outbreaks remain invisible until physical symptoms manifest on crops -- by which point substantial damage has already occurred beneath the surface.

02

Reactive, Not Proactive

Current monitoring systems respond only after visible symptoms appear, creating a perpetual catch-up cycle where interventions chase damage rather than preventing it.

03

Fragmented Surveillance

Satellite imagery and ground-level observations exist in isolation -- without integration, neither can validate the other, leaving critical blind spots in crop health assessment.

04

Vanishing Intervention Window

Effective treatment windows for crop threats are narrow. Delayed detection means delayed intervention -- often arriving after the critical period for damage mitigation has passed.

05

The Information Void

Farmers lack timely, localized, and actionable insights about their specific crop conditions -- forced to rely on generalized advice or wait until problems become visually obvious.

Systemic Barriers

Where the Signal Breaks Down

The problems persist not from lack of data or technology, but from structural disconnects in how information flows -- creating constriction points where surveillance signals fail to reach actionable outcomes.

Current Information Flow Architecture
B1

The Data Silo Divide

Satellite imagery, weather feeds, parcel boundaries, and historical pest records exist in separate systems with incompatible formats, update frequencies, and geographic references -- preventing unified analysis.

No parcel-level health index possible
B2

The Validation Vacuum

Without systematic ground-truth validation, satellite anomaly detection operates blindly. False positives go uncorrected, detection algorithms cannot learn, and confidence in alerts erodes over time.

Unverified alerts erode farmer trust
B3

The Temporal Mismatch

Weekly satellite passes, daily weather updates, and sporadic field observations operate on conflicting schedules. Without temporal alignment, real-time correlation is impossible.

Days lost between anomaly and correlation
B4

The Last-Mile Delivery Gap

Even when insights are generated, intermittent rural connectivity, language barriers, varying literacy levels, and lack of offline capability prevent alerts from reaching farmers in actionable form.

Critical window passes before farmer acts
B5

The Broken Feedback Loop

When interventions succeed or fail, that outcome data doesn't update the surveillance system. Without feedback, detection algorithms can't improve, and historical pest records remain static.

System cannot learn or self-improve
B6

The Scalability Wall

Current manual workflows and infrastructure cannot scale from pilot mandals to statewide coverage. Processing multimodal satellite data for thousands of parcels would overwhelm existing systems without automation.

Pilot insights trapped at local scale
The Compounding Effect

These six bottlenecks do not operate in isolation -- they reinforce each other. Siloed data prevents validation. Lack of validation erodes trust. Eroded trust discourages feedback. Without feedback, algorithms stagnate. Stagnant systems cannot scale. The result: a surveillance architecture where signals break down at every junction, ensuring that crop threats remain invisible until damage is done.

Village Agriculture Assistant (VAA)

Government field staff at Rythu Bharosa Kendras trusted by farmers for ground-level agricultural support. Primary mobile app users. ~10,600 VAAs across AP, each covering 2-3 villages (500-1000 households).

Validate Satellite-Detected Crop Anomaly with Ground-Truth Photo

Primary Flow
1

Opens CHSS mobile app at RBK during morning sync over Wi-Fi

Native Android app (Kotlin + Jetpack Compose) fetches assigned tasks via FastAPI REST endpoint; parcel polygons cached in Room SQLite (~30-50MB/mandal); offline maps via MapLibre + OSM vectors

VAA Mobile App (Kotlin + Room + CameraX + WorkManager)
2

Travels to parcel using offline navigation; GPS geofence confirms arrival

On-device point-in-polygon algorithm validates GPS within 50m tolerance; photo capture unlocks only when inside parcel boundary; displays crop stage and expected symptoms from PostgreSQL JSONB cache

VAA Mobile App (GPS Geofencing + Offline Maps)
3

Captures photo following on-screen guidance

TFLite MobileNetV3-Small INT8 (~3MB) validates quality in <300ms: Laplacian blur detection (>100 threshold), plant/non-plant classification, lighting check; Telugu voice feedback via pre-recorded audio if retake needed

On-Device TinyML Engine (MobileNetV3 INT8 + TFLite)
4

Confirms photo; selects symptoms from checklist; adds optional voice note

Photo compressed to WebP <200KB; GPS coordinates, timestamp, symptom codes stored in Room database; voice note encoded as Opus audio; all queued in WorkManager persistent queue

VAA Mobile App (Room + WorkManager + WebP Compression)
5

Views preliminary stress assessment instantly

Same TinyML model provides 3-class triage: green (healthy), yellow (stress detected with >70% confidence), gray (uncertain <60%); designed for high sensitivity (<10% false negatives) with broad "uncertain" category

On-Device TinyML Engine (3-Class Stress Triage)
