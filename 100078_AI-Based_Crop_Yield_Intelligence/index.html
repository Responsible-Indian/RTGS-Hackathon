Before procurement teams can plan, before MSP decisions are finalized, and before warehouses are allocated—critical yield data remains trapped in outdated estimation methods that were never designed for the speed of modern agricultural markets.

01

Crop Cutting Experiments cover only a small fraction of agricultural land. Vast regions remain unmeasured, leaving significant uncertainty in yield estimates that decision-makers must rely upon.

Root Cause Analysis

Where the Intelligence Pipeline Fractures

The problems don't exist in isolation—they persist because of deep structural constraints in how agricultural data is collected, processed, and distributed. Understanding these friction points reveals why modernization isn't optional.

B-01

The Manual Collection Bottleneck

CCE methodology requires physical field visits, manual crop cutting, and labor-intensive measurements. The sheer scale of agricultural land makes comprehensive sampling economically and logistically impossible.

Root Cause: Human throughput cannot scale to match the geographic extent of farmland requiring assessment.
B-02

Methodology Locked to Post-Harvest

By design, CCE measures actual harvested crop—meaning data collection cannot begin until the season concludes. The methodology itself precludes any form of in-season forecasting or early warning.

Root Cause: The assessment framework was built for retrospective reporting, not predictive intelligence.
B-03

Fragmented Data Ecosystems

Satellite imagery, weather data, soil information, and historical yields exist in separate systems with different formats, resolutions, and update frequencies. No unified pipeline fuses these for holistic analysis.

Root Cause: Each data source evolved independently without interoperability standards or integration infrastructure.
B-04

No Automated Processing Infrastructure

While satellite data is increasingly available, there's no operational AI/ML pipeline to automatically process multi-spectral imagery, extract vegetative indices, and translate them into actionable yield predictions.

Root Cause: Building production-grade geospatial AI systems requires specialized expertise and investment not traditionally available in agricultural departments.
B-05

The Monsoon Observation Gap

During Kharif season, persistent cloud cover blocks optical satellite sensors exactly when continuous monitoring matters most. Traditional approaches have no mechanism to see through these atmospheric barriers.

Root Cause: Optical satellites cannot penetrate clouds; multi-modal data fusion (radar, thermal) requires sophisticated processing not currently deployed.
B-06

Disconnected Decision Systems

Even when yield data exists, it doesn't flow automatically to procurement planning, MSP determination, or warehouse allocation systems. Each handoff requires manual transfer, creating delays and transcription errors.

Root Cause: Agricultural data systems and administrative decision platforms were built in isolation without API integration or shared data models.
